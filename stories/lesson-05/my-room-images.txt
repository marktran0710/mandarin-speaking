Image prompts for 《我的房間》My Room
Source: Lesson 5 – 我的錢包在哪裡？ (短文 Reading, track 05-3), mode: book
4 turns (one per sentence of the book's reading passage) → 2 columns × 2 rows.

Note: this source text is a first-person monologue, not a dialogue — every
panel is narrator-only, so all four panels take a rectangular caption box
along the bottom edge (no tail), never a speech bubble.

TEXT-FREE VERSION
═══════════════════

Single square image, divided into a clean grid of 4 equal panels (2 columns ×
2 rows), thin light gutters between panels, no hard comic-book borders, no
text, no letters, no words, no speech bubbles, no panel numbers, no captions.
Flat digital illustration style, soft rounded shapes, warm late-afternoon
color palette (cream walls, honey-coloured wood, muted sage green and soft
orange accents), limited color palette, gentle clean outlines, friendly simple
style, consistent lighting and color palette across all panels. All four
panels show the same single bedroom from different angles.

CHARACTERS AND FIXED PROPS (identical in every panel they appear in):
[THE STUDENT] a young man, about 19, wavy dark hair falling over his
forehead, slim build, plain forest-green t-shirt and grey shorts, barefoot.
[THE CAT] a small ginger tabby with white paws and a white chest.
[THE ROOM] cream walls, honey-coloured wooden floor, one wooden desk, one
neatly made single bed with a navy blanket, one sage-green two-seater sofa,
one tall bookshelf, and one large window with a thin cream curtain — the same
furniture in the same positions in every panel.

Panel 1 (top-left): wide establishing shot of the whole room from the doorway,
slightly high angle, showing the wooden desk against the left wall, the neatly
made bed along the back wall, and the sage-green sofa facing into the room on
the right; no people, warm afternoon light across the floor.

Panel 2 (top-right): medium shot at eye level from the side, a modern
flat-screen television standing dark and switched off on a low stand in front
of the sofa, and the student curled up sideways on the sofa behind it with his
knees drawn up and an open book in both hands, absorbed in reading, ignoring
the TV completely.

Panel 3 (bottom-left): close-up on the tall bookshelf and the stack of books
on the desk beside it, shot straight-on, crowded rows of colourful book spines
— some with Chinese-looking title blocks, some with Latin-alphabet-looking
title blocks (keep all titles as abstract unreadable marks, not real words); no
people in this panel.

Panel 4 (bottom-right): low wide shot from floor level, the large window with
the cream curtain pushed aside, a bright rectangle of sunlight falling across
the floorboards, and the ginger cat stretched out flat on its side in the
middle of that sunlight with its eyes closed, deeply asleep; no people.

CONSISTENCY TIPS:
- Keep the same room in all four panels — same cream walls, same honey wooden
  floor, same sage-green sofa, same furniture positions — so the four panels
  read as one room seen four ways, not four different rooms.
- Keep the cat's ginger-and-white markings identical wherever it appears.
- Vary shot framing and camera angle panel to panel (wide from the doorway /
  eye-level side view / straight-on close-up / low floor-level shot) — with no
  dialogue to carry the page, camera variety is what keeps it from reading as
  four static furniture photos.
- Keep every panel uncluttered; a plain, calm room compresses to a much
  smaller file than a busy one.

EXPORT SETTINGS (to keep the file under 1.5 MB):
- Generate at a resolution that keeps individual panels legible — e.g.
  1024 x 1024 for a 2×2 grid.
- Export as JPG (much smaller than PNG) at about 80-85% quality.
- If the file is still too large, lower JPG quality slightly, reduce overall
  dimensions, or simplify panel backgrounds.
- Use PNG only if you need transparency; if so, expect larger files and
  compress with a tool like TinyPNG to get under 1.5 MB.
