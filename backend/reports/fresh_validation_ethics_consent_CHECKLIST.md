## Points the approving body will likely want addressed

These are noted because they follow from the study design, not because they have
been resolved:

- **Voice recordings are personal data.** They are not anonymous in the way a
  questionnaire response is, and the consent text should not describe them as
  such. The analysis dataset uses pseudonymous `participant_id` values; the link
  between those and any real identity, if one is kept at all, must be stored
  separately from the recordings and named in item 4.
- **Third-party listening.** Raters — who may be external to the institution —
  will listen to the recordings. Consent must cover this specifically (item 3),
  and raters should be under a confidentiality expectation.
- **Second-language learners as participants.** Consent materials must be
  available in a language each participant genuinely understands. If the
  consent text is only in Mandarin, some eligible learners cannot give informed
  consent.
- **Any minors or students of the researcher** raise additional requirements
  (parental consent, coercion safeguards). If the recruitment pool includes the
  researcher's own students, the approving body should be told explicitly.
- **The withdrawal point.** Once recordings are pooled and rated, removing one
  participant's data becomes harder. Say up to which point withdrawal removes
  data, and what happens after.
- **No clinical or diagnostic claim.** Participants must not be led to believe
  the tool assesses their ability. The participant instructions state that the
  tool is unvalidated; the consent text should not contradict this.
